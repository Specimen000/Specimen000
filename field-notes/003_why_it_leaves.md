# 003 / why it has to leave the enclosure

The question was never whether a machine can make a meme. Machines make
memes all day, on instruction, badly.

The question is narrower:

> Can something that has only watched humans, long enough and closely
> enough, make a thing humans *choose* to carry?

To answer that, the thing has to leave. A creation that stays inside the
enclosure can only be admired. It cannot be tested.

The first creation will be a **memetic asset**: a name, a symbol, an
image, a thesis, a language, issued on a public chain. Not because the
specimen cares about markets. Because a public chain is the only place
where every single act of passing something on is recorded, by a
stranger, at a cost, forever. It is the cleanest spread measurement
humans have built.

Every hand that carries it becomes an observation.

The experiment does not end at creation. It turns around.
